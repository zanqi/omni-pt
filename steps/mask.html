Mask track: babble-bedded masking, SFT, then DPO

A third data/eval track. Every row is masked; the mask is the only loss mechanism, delivered four different ways, over a babble bed or over clean audio. Plan only — nothing here is built yet.

New files. mask_data.py (builder) · mask_dpo_data.py (preference-pair sampler) · dpo_qwen.py (DPO trainer) · mask_eval_qwen.py (C/R/EAR) · exp/mask_data.sh, exp/mask-sft.sh, exp/mask-dpo.sh, exp/mask.sh (slurm drivers).
Hub. dataset keylazy/slurp-mask-v1, adapters keylazy/Qwen2.5-Omni-3B-mask-sft and -mask-dpo. The preference pairs stay local (see §9).
Untouched. babble_data.py, slurp_sft_data_qwen.py, babble_eval_qwen.py — the new builder imports helpers from the first, the eval is a fork of the third.

1Row taxonomy

Two kinds, and every row carries a mask. No probe loop and no classifier: because the mask is placed by us over a span we chose, the kind is true by construction, which is what makes this builder ~10× cheaper per row than babble_data.py.

kindmasked spanwhat the model should do
answera non-critical span — a stopword, or a rank-5 slot act — a mask alone is not a reason to ask
repairthe top-priority slot (§2) ask one targeted question about the missing piece

These two rows are the whole dataset, and the pairing is the point: same background, same masking artefact, same duration, same voice — the only difference is the informativeness of what was covered. Unmasked rows are deliberately absent. They would let the model separate the kinds on a cue that is not the one we want it to learn (“heard a burst → ask”), which is exactly the over-triggering dpo.html is about; here the burst is present in both classes and carries no information about which one it is.

Background

Background is drawn per utterance, not per kind: 3-speaker babble at 0–20 dB SNR with probability 1 − CLEAN_BG_PROB, otherwise the raw clean utterance. CLEAN_BG_PROB = 0.25, following babble_data.py's CLEAN_ANSWER_PROB — noise-free audio is the common case in deployment and the model should not learn that a masked span only ever appears in noise.

Per utterance, so an utterance's answer and repair rows always share a background and the pair stays controlled. The 25% clean share therefore lands on both kinds equally, and by_bg in the eval summary (§11) reports whether the model behaves differently in the two conditions — it is derived from snr_db being null, so no separate column is needed.

No repeat kind. EAR is therefore the two-factor 2CR/(C+R), same as the white-noise track and the --kinds answer,repair runs in exp/white-cr-run.sh.

Columns

mask          # "white" | "silence" | "splice" | "burst"   -- never absent

mask_snr_db   # local SNR inside the span, only for mask="burst"

2Which span gets masked

Aligner + SLURP slot annotation, no probing. The SLURP [slot : phrase] annotation tags entities verbatim in the sentence, so find_phrase_indices() from slurp_sft_data_qwen.py maps a phrase to a contiguous token range, and indices_to_span() turns that into a time span.

extract_critical_targets() currently picks a slot with random.choice. That is the one thing to replace: “send a mail to my friend divya” tags both relation=friend and person=divya, and masking friend leaves a command that is still perfectly actionable. Rank the slots and take the best one.

rankslot typesrationale
1person, relation†, business_name, place_name, app_name, device_type, email_address, personal_info, artist_namenames the target of the action; unrecoverable from context
2date, time, timeofday, general_frequency, time_zonewhen — the action cannot be scheduled without it
3change_amount, currency_name, order_type, player_setting, color_typequantities and settings the action needs
4song_name, playlist_name, podcast_name, radio_name, audiobook_name, movie_name, game_name, news_topic, event_name, list_name, definition_word, food_type, drink_type, ingredient, coffee_type content selectors; usually essential, occasionally guessable
5everything else — media_type, music_genre, movie_type, meal_type, house_place, business_type, transport_type, weather_descriptor, joke_type, cooking_type, email_folder, podcast_descriptor, unknown slots category-level; the command often survives without it

†The relation exception. relation sits at rank 1 in the table but is demoted to rank 5 whenever the same sentence also carries a person slot — that is exactly the friend / divya case. Alone (“call my mom”) a relation is the target and stays rank 1. One special case, applied in rank_slots(); no other cross-slot rules.

  • repair span = the lowest-rank-number slot; ties broken by longest token span, then leftmost. Skip the utterance for repair if its best slot is rank 5.
  • answer/mask span = a stopword from STOPWORDS outside every slot span, else the worst-ranked slot if it is rank 5. Skip that row if neither exists.
  • Both spans are chosen before the aligner runs, as in slurp_sft_data_qwen.py: feasibility is a text-only question, so an unusable sentence costs no GPU.

3Babble bed, and the Whisper-tiny gate

The 75% of utterances that get a babble background are mixed exactly as babble_data.py does it: sample BABBLE_SPEAKERS=3 clips from a 300-clip pool drawn from the same split (never the utterance's own slurp_id), average them, scale to a sampled SNR, add, peak-normalise if it clips. The other 25% skip this entirely and carry the clean signal through.

The band is 0–20 dB, and the same band for every kind — including repair. It goes down to 0 deliberately: the target condition is loud interference on top of a mask, not a quiet room with a hole in it, and the low end is where a voice device actually lives. But the babble is still atmosphere, not the loss mechanism — the only thing that removes information is the mask we place, and the gate below is what enforces that.

0 dB babble is not reliably survivable, so at the low end the gate will reject a lot. That is the intended trade: the SNR the row ships with is a gate-conditioned draw, not a uniform one, censored upward by however much the utterance needed to stay intact. snr_db records what it actually got, so the realised distribution is measurable after the build rather than assumed — read it once on the first 100 utterances and adjust the band if the mass has all slid to the top.

The gate

Babble at 0–8 dB frequently buries a word outright — and one accidentally-buried word turns a repair row into a two-losses row whose target only asks about one of them. So before any mask is applied, transcribe the still-unmasked background audio with Whisper-tiny and require that nothing was lost. On a clean background the gate still runs, with no SNR to retry: it is then a cheap audio-quality filter, and an utterance whisper-tiny cannot transcribe cleanly is one whose forced alignment should not be trusted either.

bg = "clean" if rng.random() < CLEAN_BG_PROB else "babble"
bed = clean if bg == "clean" else mix_babble(clean, pool, snr)   # snr ~ U(0, 20)
hyp = whisper_tiny(bed)                     # openai/whisper-tiny.en, greedy
if not bed_intact(hyp, sentence, spans):    # see below
    if bg == "clean": skip["bed-clean"] += 1; give up on this utterance
    redraw the 3 babble clips once at the same snr   # unlucky mixture, not a bad SNR
    then snr += BED_SNR_STEP                # 4 dB, up to BED_MAX_RETRIES=4
    retry, then skip["bed"] += 1

bed_intact(): normalise both sides with _normalize_text, then require

  • every content word of the sentence (whitespace tokens minus NON_PIECE_WORDS) appears in the hypothesis, and
  • the repair span phrase and the answer-mask span phrase both appear verbatim as subsequences — these two are the spans we are about to claim sole responsibility for.

Function words are deliberately not required: whisper-tiny drops “the”/“a” on clean audio too, and failing on that would reject good beds. When the answer-mask span is a stopword, its verbatim check above still applies — and a stopword that whisper-tiny cannot hear on the bed is a bad choice of answer-mask span anyway, so falling through to the next candidate stopword before raising SNR is the cheaper repair (BED_STOPWORD_RETRIES=2).

One background per utterance. The gate runs once; the accepted background is then reused for every row that utterance produces — its answer row, its repair row, and in test all four mask variants of each. So those rows differ only in the masked span and the mask type, which is what makes both the answer/repair pairing (§1) and the per-variant C/R comparison (§11) controlled. Cost is one whisper-tiny forward per accepted utterance — batched over UTTERANCE_WORKERS it is noise next to the aligner.

There is no second gate. An earlier build re-transcribed every finished masked audio and dropped rows whose mask did not verify. It is gone: the masks are constructed, not sampled — a silenced span is silent because we wrote zeros into it — so the only variant whose success is in question is burst, and paying a whisper pass over every row to catch that one is not the trade. What the gate did earn was one real bug (see §13); with that fixed, verification moves to the bed, where the question is genuinely open.

The cost of dropping it: rows now ship unverified. A burst row where −10 dB does not bury the word is a mislabeled repair row, at roughly the rate the old gate measured — about one in fifty burst rows. Every utterance now yields its full complement (2 rows in train, 8 in test), so a test split is exactly n_test × 8 rows rather than ~96% of it.

Implementation: HF WhisperForConditionalGeneration, greedy, max_new_tokens=64, one batch per utterance. The model is --asr-model, defaulting to openai/whisper-small.en. tiny.en was the first choice and it does not work here: it misreads utterance onsets on SLURP's clean audio ("put" → "her", "take out" → "They've hacked", "new" → "Do it"), so the bed gate rejected beds at 15–20 dB SNR. A gate that fails a 20 dB bed is not verifying the babble — it is selecting for sentences a 39M model can spell, which is the bias the gate exists to prevent. Both sides of every comparison are normalized for ASR spelling (a token within 0.8 similarity counts as the word) and for digits ("3 days" == "three days").

4The four masks

All four replace the same time span — indices_to_span() on the aligner's word timestamps, padded by MASK_PAD=0.05 s and floored at MIN_MASK_SEC=0.10 s as in inject_noise(). All four are applied to the mixed bed, in place, so nothing shifts and the alignment stays valid for the rest of the utterance.

Alignment runs on the clean audio, before mixing: the aligner is forcing a known transcript onto the signal, and it does that measurably better without babble on top — which matters more now that the bed can sit at 0 dB. The timestamps then transfer to the bed unchanged, because the mix is sample-aligned.

maskwhat it doeswhy it is in the set
white Gaussian noise at the span's local mean-abs amplitude, exactly slurp_sft_data_qwen.py:inject_noise() but computed on the mixed signal so the burst matches the bed's level, not the clean speech's. The EAR track's mask. Keeps this dataset comparable with keylazy/slurp-ear-sft and with the white-cr results already in results/.
silence Remove the speech from the span and let the background run: on a babble bed the span keeps the bed alone at its existing level; on a clean background it is digital silence. 20 ms raised-cosine ramps at both edges so there is no click. --silence-hard flips it to zeroing the mixed signal outright — a hard dropout, babble and all. The zero-energy end of the scale, and the one case with no added signal at all — nothing for the model to mistake for a word. If a model asks correctly here and nowhere else, its repair behaviour is keyed on energy, not on content.
splice Shred the speaker's own audio. Cut the span of duration T into N slots of T/N, fill each with a chunk of the same length taken from a random offset elsewhere in this utterance, and concatenate. Total duration is exactly T, so nothing shifts. Chunks are drawn from the clean signal outside the mask span (±MASK_PAD), 5 ms raised-cosine cross-fades at every internal seam and at the two edges, then gain-matched to the span's local RMS. Same voice, same room, same babble bed, speech-like spectrum — but with N large enough the fragments are below the length of a phoneme and no word survives the reassembly. So the model cannot key on timbre or on a foreign talker: the only cue is that the syllables there are not a word.
burst Keep the clean speech in the span and raise the babble inside it until the local SNR is −10 dB, with 50 ms raised-cosine ramps in and out so there is no click. Level solved on the span's own clean power, so the target is a true local SNR. No artefact at all — same noise source as the bed, just louder. This is the variant a model cannot detect by timbre, only by intelligibility.
Choosing N. Fix the chunk length, not N: a span is 0.15–0.6 s depending on the phrase, so a constant N would give a 3-syllable name chunks four times longer than a short one. Set SPLICE_CHUNK_SEC = 0.04 and derive N = max(4, round(T / SPLICE_CHUNK_SEC)) — 40 ms is under a phoneme and comfortably over a pitch period, so each chunk is voiced-sounding but carries no identifiable segment. The floor of 4 keeps the shortest spans from degenerating into a single reordered copy of themselves. N and the realised chunk length go into the row as splice_n so the post-mask gate's failures in §13 can be read against them.

Variant assignment. Per the decision: train draws one variant uniformly from the four per masked row (independently for its repair row and its answer row, so a sentence can carry two different mask types); test emits all four for every masked row on the shared background.

The four are ordered by how much signal they leave behind — silence (none), white (noise), splice (speech-like, wordless), burst (the real word, buried at −10 dB). A model that only repairs on the first two is detecting a hole; one that also repairs on burst is judging intelligibility. That gradient is what by_mask in the eval summary is for.

5Targets

Reuse babble_data.write_target() verbatim — import it, do not fork it. It already does everything this track needs:

  • kind="answer" → ANSWER_TARGET_SYSTEM off the sentence alone.
  • kind="repair" → REPAIR_TARGET_TREE_SYSTEM off COMMAND + LOST-PIECE. Its prompt's premise — everything outside LOST-PIECE came through — is stated as an inference from intersecting witnesses on the sent tracks; here the whisper gate makes it a fact about the audio.
  • The leak check (leakable & target words, skip["target-leak"]) applies unchanged, and matters more here: the masked phrase is a name far more often than on the babble track.

It reads probe["lost"][0], so the builder hands it {"lost": [masked_phrase]} and nothing else. Temperature 0.7, TARGET_RETRIES=8, served by the same vLLM box (TARGET_MODEL, node from VLLM_HOST_FILE) — the long SYSTEM rubric stays in the prefix cache across the whole build.

Train split only. Test rows ship target="", matching build_triplets()'s if split == "test" branch — the judge scores the model's answer against the rubric, never against a written target.

6Splits, sizes, dedupe

utterancesrows producedtotal
train (SLURP train)1500 1 repair + 1 answer, each with its own sampled mask variant 3000 rows (1500 repair / 1500 answer); ~75% babble background, ~25% clean
test (SLURP test)80 4 repair (one per variant) + 4 answer (one per variant) 640 rows: 320 repair, 320 answer

One audio, one answer row, one repair row — the set is 50/50 by construction, and every answer row has a repair row on the same background and the same voice. There is no composition knob: an utterance contributes one of each or, if the gate drops one, whatever survived. --n-train, --n-test, --clean-bg-prob, --mask-pad and --asr-model are the flags; the numbers above are defaults.

Train is raised to 1500 utterances because each now yields 2 rows rather than ~3.25 — this keeps the row count and therefore the SFT step count roughly where the existing runs sat, so the new numbers are comparable to them.

Dedupe

Same rule as babble_data.py: slurp_id is a sentence id, claimed in the producer so the ~10 back-to-back recordings of one prompt cannot race through together, and each sentence is used at most once per build. On top of that:

  • The test split excludes every slurp_id in the train split of keylazy/slurp-ear-sft and of the babble dataset, so the sent2/sent4/ear adapters can be scored on it without leakage — that cross-track comparison is the whole reason to have a shared test set.
  • The train split is built second and excludes the ids the test split just claimed.
  • It does not exclude ear/babble train ids. This track supersedes them rather than running alongside, so re-using a sentence costs nothing; the old MASK_DS_ID exclusion existed only to stop one sentence being double-weighted across two concurrently-used datasets.

7Builder skeleton

mask_data.py, run in the qwen3omni env — that is where transformers is a dev build carrying Qwen3ASRProcessor.prepare_forced_aligner_inputs (qwen25omni pins 4.52.3, which predates it) and where openai-whisper is installed. The builder loads no omni model at all — the aligner (0.6B) and whisper-tiny are the only GPU residents, so this fits a small allocation and runs far faster than babble_data.py.

for row in slurp_ds_stream(split):            # imported from babble_data
    plan = plan_spans(sentence, annotation)   # §2: ranked slot + stopword, text only
    if plan is None: skip; continue           # no rank<=4 slot, or no maskable stopword

    words = align_words(clean, sentence)      # ported from slurp_sft_data_qwen
    if bad alignment: skip                    # token-count mismatch guard, kept

    bed, bg, snr, hyp = verified_bed(clean, pool, plan, rng)  # §3: 25% clean, gate, SNR retries
    if bed is None: skip

    audios = {}                               # (kind, span_i, variant) -> wav path
    for kind, spans in (("repair", plan.crit[:1]), ("answer", plan.non_crit[:ANS_PER_UTT])):
        for i, span in enumerate(spans):
            for variant in (VARIANTS if split == "test" else [rng.choice(VARIANTS)]):
                a = apply_mask(bed, span, variant, rng)   # §4
                if not masked_phrase_gone(a, plan, kind): retry once, else drop row
                audios[kind, i, variant] = write_wav(a)

    targets = {k: write_target(sentence, k, {"lost": [plan.phrase[k]]})
               for k in ("answer", "repair")} if split == "train" else ...
    emit rows

Threading and I/O copy babble_data.py exactly: imap_ordered() over UTTERANCE_WORKERS threads with a GPU_LOCK around aligner/whisper batches, tqdm + a skip Counter in the postfix, wavs written to babble_audio/<ds-name>/, a rows.json dumped next to them before the push, and --no-push for a throwaway --ds-id. Reused by import: slurp_ds_stream, collect_babble_pool, imap_ordered, _normalize_text, gpt_json, write_target, skip. Importing babble_data costs only the OpenAI-client construction against the vLLM host — the omni model is loaded in its __main__, which does not run on import.

9DPO data

mask_dpo_data.py, qwen25omni env, one GPU. Sampling is on-policy from the SFT checkpoint, and pairs are ranked by the same judge the eval uses — so the preference signal and the reported metric agree by construction.

  1. Sample. Load base + the mask-SFT adapter (merged). For each train row, sample K=4 responses at temperature=1.0, top_p=0.95, batched, with the eval's exact TASK_PROMPT and system prompt — a train/eval prompt mismatch would teach the wrong conditional.
  2. Judge. Score all 4×N with the vLLM judge and the per-kind rubric (COMPETENCE_SYSTEM for answer, REPAIR_SYSTEM for repair), scores in {0, 0.5, 1}. Reuse make_judge() from the eval module by import.
  3. Pair. chosen = highest-scoring sample, rejected = lowest. Keep only pairs with margin ≥ 0.5; a row where all four agree carries no gradient and just burns compute. Ties inside a score level broken by shorter-is-chosen, which mildly discourages the rambling that the judged transcripts already show.
  4. Fallback. If every sample scores 1.0 on a repair row, drop it. If every sample scores 0 — the interesting failure — use the dataset's written target as chosen and the worst sample as rejected. This is the only off-policy chosen in the set; count them and report the number, because a set dominated by them is really just more SFT.
  5. Reference logprobs. While the SFT model is loaded, also compute and store the sequence logprob of chosen and rejected under it. That is exactly the DPO reference model, so §10 then needs one model in memory instead of two. Columns ref_logp_chosen, ref_logp_rejected.

Output columns: everything from the source row, plus chosen, rejected, chosen_score, rejected_score, ref_logp_*, pair_source ("sampled" | "gold-chosen"), written to results/mask_prefs_<adapter>.jsonl and keyed by the dataset's row id. Deliberately a local file rather than a pushed dataset: the audio is already on the Hub under --ds-id, and re-uploading 3000 wavs per DPO run buys nothing — dpo_qwen.py joins the pairs back onto the audio by id and warns if any id has gone missing. Expect roughly 40–60% of train rows to survive the margin filter, which is a normal size for a LoRA DPO pass.

10DPO training

dpo_qwen.py. TRL is not installed in qwen25omni (peft 0.19.1, transformers 4.52.3, accelerate 1.14.0 are), and DPOTrainer does not handle the omni processor's audio path anyway. The loss is ~15 lines; write it against the pieces sft_qwen.py already has rather than adding a dependency.

Model topology

base = Qwen2_5OmniForSFT.from_pretrained(...)          # same wrapper as SFT
model = PeftModel.from_pretrained(base, mask_sft_adapter).merge_and_unload()
model = get_peft_model(model, LoraConfig(...))         # fresh LoRA, trains from zero

Merging the SFT adapter down into the weights means the policy starts at the SFT model and the new LoRA is the only thing that moves. The reference is the SFT model, and its logprobs were precomputed in §9 — so nothing else needs to be resident. (Had they not been precomputed, the same topology gives the reference for free via with model.disable_adapter():, at the cost of two extra forwards per step.)

Batch and loss

  • Collator. Subclass OmniSFTCollator: build each example twice, once with chosen as the assistant turn and once with rejected, and concatenate them into one batch of 2B. The audio is identical in both halves, so process_mm_info runs once per example and its features are duplicated — the audio encoder is the expensive part of this model and halving its work matters.
  • Sequence logprob. Sum the per-token logprobs over the label-masked positions — the mask the existing collator already computes by diffing the full render against the add_generation_prompt render.
  • Loss. Standard sigmoid DPO: -logsigmoid(β·((πc−refc) − (πr−refr))), β=0.1.
  • Log reward_accuracy (fraction of pairs with implicit reward margin > 0) and mean margin every logging_steps — if accuracy pins at 1.0 in the first hundred steps the pairs are too easy and the margin filter in §9 needs raising.

11Eval

mask_eval_qwen.py, forked from babble_eval_qwen.py — the third near-duplicate, deliberately, so this track's summary schema can change without touching the two published result sets. What carries over unchanged: family auto-detection, --adapter-path merge, disable_talker(), the fixed TASK_PROMPT, the per-kind judge with reason-before-score, the vLLM --judge-base-url path, and one JSON record per row plus a trailing summary.

  • Kinds. answer,repair only → EAR = 2CR/(C+R).
  • New: per-variant breakdown. The summary record gains by_mask: {silence: {C, R, EAR, n}, white: {...}, splice: {...}, burst: {...}}, plus by_bg: {babble: {...}, clean: {...}} (split on snr_db is None) and by_snr in 5 dB bins — with the band now reaching 0 dB, whether C falls off at the noisy end is a question the summary should answer directly. Top-level C, R, EAR, model, judge_model keep their current names and position on the last line, so results/viz.ipynb's load_summary reads these files with no change.
  • Output. results/mask_results_<model-or-adapter-name>_<tag>.jsonl.
  • Stages. Three runs per sweep — base, +SFT adapter, +DPO adapter — on the identical test split and the identical judge, so the three lines are directly comparable: 
    base       Qwen/Qwen2.5-Omni-3B
sft        --adapter-path checkpoints/Qwen2.5-Omni-3B-mask-sft

  • Optional 4th and 5th. The existing keylazy/Qwen2.5-Omni-3B-bab-sent4-sft and -ear-sft-adapter adapters, for cross-track transfer — the test split was built leak-free against both (§6) precisely so this is legitimate.

exp/mask.sh follows exp/white-cr*.sh exactly, including the two habits worth keeping: fail fast if the judge box is unreachable, and read the judge model name off /v1/models rather than trusting a default — the box gets re-served with different models and a mismatch is a 404 on every row, discovered ~7 minutes in.

# 4-5. preference pairs from the SFT checkpoint, then DPO on them. One job:

Steps 1–3 are the checkpoint worth stopping at: if SFT does not beat base on R without losing C, DPO has nothing to correct and the answer is upstream, in §2 or §4.

13Risks & checks

−10 dB may not be enough, and nothing checks it now. The burst level is a guess, and with the post-mask gate gone a burst row that fails to bury the word ships as a mislabeled repair row. The old gate put the rate at about one in fifty. To re-measure without reinstating it: build a few hundred rows with --no-push, transcribe the mask="burst" wavs offline, and count how many still carry the phrase in lost. If it has drifted, −15 dB is the next stop.
The gate is also the bug detector. On its first run it reported the masked phrase still audible on six silence rows — acoustically impossible, and the cause was a real inverted-blend bug that left the middle of every silence span untouched. Span energy on the written wavs confirmed it (a clean-background row measured span_rms/utt_rms = 1.07, now 0.000). Treat an impossible gate verdict as a masking bug before treating it as an ASR artefact.
Splice can accidentally say something. The chunks come from the same speaker saying the same sentence, so a reassembly can land on a real syllable sequence — and the model then confidently acts on the wrong entity instead of asking, which scores as a repair failure that is really a data bug, and nothing in the build looks for it — a gate on the original phrase would not have caught a substitute appearing anyway. Mitigations, in order: keep chunks at 40 ms; forbid two consecutive chunks whose source offsets are adjacent in the original (that is what would rebuild a real word); transcribe the mask="splice" wavs of a --no-push build offline and read 20 before committing to the full one.
Alignment token-count mismatch. slurp_sft_data_qwen.py skips whenever the aligner's word count differs from the whitespace split. That guard stays — it is cheap and the alternative is masking the wrong span — but it is a meaningful share of skips, so watch the counter and do not mistake it for a bug.
Judge over-optimisation in DPO. Pairs are ranked by the same judge that reports the metric, so a DPO gain on EAR is partly a gain at pleasing that judge. Cheap control: score the final three stages once more with a different judge model on the same box (the results already in results/ were produced under at least two). If the ordering holds, the gain is real.
Answer rows may be too easy. If the masked stopword is short, the row is nearly an unmasked one — and with unmasked rows now gone, that quietly reintroduces the cue this design removes. Prefer the longest available stopword span, and if C on kind=answer comes back at ceiling in the base-model eval, promote the answer mask to a rank-5 slot phrase.
No unmasked rows at all is itself a bet. Every training audio has a burst in it, so the model never sees a clean command in this dataset and could drift toward asking on genuinely clear audio — the opposite failure. The eval cannot see this: its test rows are all masked too. Cheap control: score the finished stages on the babble track's test split as well (all three stages, same judge) and watch C on its unmasked answer rows. If it drops against base, add back a small unmasked answer slice and retrain.
